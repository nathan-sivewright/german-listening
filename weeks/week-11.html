Week 11

Money & Banking

Understand your bank statement or the Pfand system.

1101 Mein Taschengeld
NEURAL2-F
Speed
  1. How much pocket money do Lena and Paul get each week?
  2. What is Lena saving for, and why does she need it?
  3. How did Lena get three euros last Saturday?
  4. What does Paul spend his money on, and what happens at the end of the month?
1102 Am Pfandautomaten
NEURAL2-F NEURAL2-B
Speed
  1. Why does Mehmet have so many bags with him?
  2. What is the problem, and what does Clara suggest?
  3. How much does Mehmet get for a glass bottle, and how much for plastic bottles and cans?
  4. What can Mehmet do with the receipt from the machine?
1103 Die Karte ist weg!
NEURAL2-F NEURAL2-B
Speed
  1. Why is Daniel calling, and when did he last use his card?
  2. What information does Frau Kraus ask for?
  3. When will the new card arrive, how much does it cost, and what does Daniel think of the price?
  4. How can Daniel get cash until then, and what tip does Frau Kraus give him?
1104 Spartipps im Radio
NEURAL2-B
Speed
  1. How much does a lunch cost in a restaurant, and how much at home?
  2. Which way of getting around is the most expensive, and which is the cheapest?
  3. Why should you shop in the evening, and why should you read your bank statement?
  4. What is the presenter's favourite tip, and how does Frau Schmid save money?
1105 Die WG-Kasse
NEURAL2-F NEURAL2-B
Speed
  1. What did Sophie, Ben and Jana each pay for at the party?
  2. How much does each person pay in the end, and how much does Ben owe Sophie?
  3. What is the problem with Jana, and why don't they talk to her tonight?
  4. What do they decide about the shared kitty, and what will Ben still buy at the organic shop?
1106 Opa Georg erzählt
NEURAL2-B
Speed
  1. What was Georg's job, and how did his salary compare with salaries today?
  2. What was cheaper back then, and what was expensive?
  3. How did Georg get his salary, and how did he and his wife save money?
  4. How does his granddaughter pay today, and why does Georg still prefer cash?
This week focuses on comparison: the comparative (billiger als, besser), the superlative (am billigsten, am liebsten) and so … wie. Each script is embedded directly in its matching lesson (1101 → Lesson 1, 1102 → Lesson 2, etc.).
